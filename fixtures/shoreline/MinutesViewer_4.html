A.  Public Hearing on Ordinance No. 1051 - Extension of Interim Regulations Prohibiting the Removal of Trees Measuring 60 Inches Diameter at Breast Height or Greater Within the Town Center 3 and Town Center 4 Zoning Districts (30 Minutes)

 
Staff Report - Ordinance No. 1051, Tree Removal
 
Rachel Weber
 
Presentation - Extension Interim Regs 1051
 
Jonelle Kemmerling
 
Nancy Morris
 
Sierra Carson, Senior Planner, presented Ordinance No. 1051, which would extend, for six months, the interim regulations currently prohibiting the removal of trees 60 inches in diameter at breast height or greater within the Town Center 3 and Town Center 4 zone districts. The current interim regulation is set to expire on January 27, 2026. She noted the City is planning for permanent regulations to be in place by the end of 2026 and interim regulations are needed until then.

Mayor Roberts opened the public hearing.

Boni Beiry, Shoreline Tree Code Preservation Team, requested the regulation be extended and expanded citywide. And she emphasized the benefits of trees on air quality and stormwater control.

Mayor Roberts closed the public hearing.

Seeing no further discussion, Mayor Roberts stated the item is scheduled to be returned for Council action on January 5th.

 

B.  Action on Resolution No. 558 - Amending the 2025 and 2026 Fee Schedules Regarding Permitted Hazardous Tree Removal (15 Minutes)
- Staff Report
- Public Comment
- Council Action

 
Staff Report - Resolution No. 558, Hazardous Trees
 
Kathleen Russell
 
Sierra Carson, Senior Planner, explained Resolution No. 558, which proposes a fee exemption for the removal of extremely hazard trees 24 inches in diameter at breast height or greater. She commented on applicant issues and burdens regarding the fee for removing large hazardous trees. She also emphasized tracking tree removals through the permitting process.

Mayor Roberts opened the public hearing. 

Kathleen Russell, Shoreline, spoke in support of Resolution No. 558 to reduce the burden on applicants for removal of hazardous trees.

Patricia Weber, Shoreline, spoke against Resolution No. 558 because it weakens Shoreline’s position on maintaining the tree canopy.

Mayor Roberts closed the public hearing. 
 
Councilmember Eben Pobee moved to approve Resolution No. 558. Councilmember Annette Ademasu seconded the motion, which carried 6-0.
 
Councilmembers commented on the balance of maintaining trees and supporting applicants’ needs for permitted hazardous tree removal. Councilmember Scully said he is not in favor of including a three tree per year exemption in the fee structure. And he is hopeful that tree code updates will strike a better balance between tree preservation and property rights.
 

C.  Action on Ordinance No. 1045 - Amending Shoreline Municipal Code Chapters 20.20, 20.30, 20.50, and 20.80 Reflecting Updates to the Critical Areas Regulations (30 minutes)

 
Staff Report - Ordinance No. 1045, Critical Areas
 
Bob Danson
 
Lora Petso
 
Elise Keim, Planning Manager, reviewed the main updates of the Critical Areas regulations, highlighting the improved clarity on native species requirements and tracking of one-time expansions. The new riparian management zone is 200 feet wide, increasing the amount of regulated critical land in the city. And a draft amendment was shared to clarify the definition of physically separated and functionally isolated areas to help show what is regulated as critical area.

Ms. Keim shared that Olympic View Water and Sewer District requested the City designate wellhead protection area buffers as critical aquifer recharge areas and adopt their Watershed Protection Plan Guidelines. Staff proposed another solution to add a wellhead protection area buffer section to the stormwater code. Ms. Keim said the City and Olympic View are working collaboratively on the best resolution and aim to enter an interim memorandum of understanding to establish protections.
 
Councilmember Keith Scully moved to adopt Ordinance No. 1045 as shown in Attachment A of tonight's report. Councilmember Eben Pobee seconded the motion, which carried 6-0.
 
Councilmember Scully expressed hesitation about potentially unnecessary regulations impacting residential properties in the wellhead protection area. He encouraged staff to err on the side of caution and asked about water quality issues in need of addressing. Tricia Juhnke, Public Works Director, said she believes Olympic View’s concerns are with PFAS contamination in underground injection control areas. However, there is not much known about how to appropriately treat PFAS.
 
Deputy Mayor Laura Mork moved to amend the Planning Commission’s recommendation to amend SMC 20.80.320(F) and 20.80.420(G) as shown in tonight’s staff report. Councilmember Keith Scully seconded the motion, which carried 6-0.
 
Council discussed with staff various types of critical areas and options to protect them. Councilmember Scully commented that Olympic View’s recommendations seem reasonable and he would defer to their utility expertise where it makes sense. The Council agreed to revisit the wellhead protection area regulations in the first quarter of 2026.
 

9.  STUDY ITEMS

 

A.  Discussion of the 2024 Annual Traffic Report (30 Minutes)

 
Staff Report - 2024 Traffic Report
 
Jacqueline Kurle
 
Oliver Moffat
 
Allison Luzader
 
Rachel Weber
 
Katie Lorah
 
Kevin Atkinson.
 
Peggy Douglas
 
Tom Lang
 
Jonelle Kemmerling
 
Presentation - Traffic Report
 
Kendra Dedinsky, Transportation Manager, said the Annual Traffic Report is the City’s method to analyze crash and traffic data to make informed decisions about prioritizing safety improvements and traffic enforcement. She noted the City is currently conducting a Strategic Safety Plan which will wrap up by the end of 2026. She provided a disclaimer, noting that additional locations with shared jurisdictional responsibility have been added to the analysis. This resulted in higher numbers of crashes than in previous years.

Ms. Dedinsky reported total crashes have increased but fatal and serious injury crashes have dipped. She reviewed crash data including the locations and any improvements made or planned for these locations. She noted signal timing changes, speed limit reductions, and other safety efforts implemented in the last year and a half. She reviewed parking, traffic and DUI citations, speeding data, and traffic volumes and said staff plan to present an automated enforcement policy for Council review in first quarter 2026.

Councilmember Pobee said he was surprised by the increase in pedestrian injury and asked what the factors were. Ms. Dedinsky said speed is always a primary factor, as well as distracted driving and driving under the influence. She talked about targeting these behaviors with enforcement and positive reinforcement education campaigns.

Councilmember Ramsdell stated bike infrastructure should allow cyclists of all levels to feel safe and that he would like to see 185th Street have protected bike lanes in the future. He asked about citations for parking the wrong direction. Mr. Norris confirmed parking the wrong direction is a violation, but tickets are getting administratively dismissed because there is a typo in the code citation on the ticket. The City will be issuing warnings for now until the typo is corrected.

Councilmember Ademasu said she is curious about eBike crash data, additional signal timing projects, and speeding in school zones. Captain Brian Angelo explained the process of data collection at an accident scene and the subsequent reporting. He added school zones are a hard area to enforce and write tickets at, but they do their best.

Mayor Roberts asked for an update on the Police Traffic Unit. Captain Angelo said two officers were added, with one dedicated to DUI. However, there are still staffing issues, and a very high volume of calls, so traffic emphasis is still a challenge.

Mayor Roberts said he would like the City to continue to focus on reducing speeds to try to reduce fatalities down to zero.

At 9:25 p.m., Mayor Roberts announced that Council is recessing into an Executive Session for a period of 10 minutes as authorized by RCW 42.30.110(1)(i) to discuss with legal counsel potential litigation to which the City, or a member acting in an official capacity is, or is likely to become, a party. Councilmember John Ramsdell moved to authorize the City Manager to pursue all appropriate legal remedies, including the initiation of litigation, to challenge the legality of the unilateral U.S. Department of Transportation Second Amended and Restated Grant Agreement Under the Fiscal Year 2023 RAISE Program for the previously executed RAISE Grant for the West Side Transformation Multimodal Connections to the Shoreline South Regional Transit Hub. Councilmember Eben Pobee seconded the motion, which carried 6-0.
